Digital Usability and Web Routines Study

You are invited to take part in a study on digital habits, how people use web interfaces, and how they interact with everyday websites. The study is part of a bachelor's thesis in Psychology and Cognitive Sciences.

What you will do

You will use a web page and answer a few short sets of questions about your online routines. It takes about 5 minutes.

Your answers

The questions that ask for personal details are optional, and you can leave any of them blank. The rating questions need an answer before you can continue. You can stop at any time by closing this tab. Nothing is saved unless you reach the end and confirm.

What is stored

Only numbers and multiple-choice answers are stored. Nothing you type into a text box is stored or sent anywhere, and your name is never asked for. Results are analysed in aggregate.

Technical information

Like any website, this page can read standard information your browser shares (for example device type, screen size and approximate location based on your IP address). To show you this information later, the page looks up your IP address with a third-party service (ipapi.co). Your final answers are sent to a Google Sheets form, which means Google also receives your IP address when you submit. Neither your IP address nor your device details are stored in the study data.

You have already taken part

    Why these details matter

    Your email address is usually the starting point for resetting your other accounts. A first pet, a school and a childhood street are classic answers to security questions. Many large services no longer rely on these questions alone, but some banks, older accounts and customer support lines still accept them. Together with a birth year, these details also make phishing messages far more convincing, because an attacker can mention things only you should know.

    What your browser shared on its own

    No permission prompt appeared, yet any website can read the details below. They are not stored in the study data.

    Why this matters

    None of these details identifies you on its own. Combined, they can form a browser fingerprint that is often distinctive enough for tracking networks to recognise the same device across different websites, even after cookies are cleared.

    What you can do

    • Treat security questions like passwords. Give made-up answers and keep them in a password manager, so nobody can look them up or guess them.
    • Protect your email first. Turn on two-factor authentication, ideally with an authenticator app or a passkey rather than SMS. Your email can unlock most of your other accounts.
    • Be wary of sites that ask for personal details to "check" your security. A legitimate breach checker, such as Have I Been Pwned, only needs your email address.
    • Reduce fingerprinting. Browsers such as Firefox, Safari and Brave include protections that make your device harder to track across sites.

    About this study

    What we were studying

    This study looks at the "privacy paradox": people often say their privacy matters to them, yet share personal details easily online. We wanted to know whether a website that presents itself as protecting you makes people share more than a plain survey does, and how seeing their own data changes how vulnerable people feel.

    Participants were randomly assigned to one of two versions.

    What was not true

      Why we could not tell you at the start

      If people know a study is about sharing personal information, they share differently, and the results would no longer reflect everyday behaviour. Withholding this was approved for this study because no real harm results and everything is explained here, before any data is kept.

      What is kept, and what is not

      If you agree below, we keep only: which version you saw, whether each personal-detail box was filled in (not what you typed), your multiple-choice answers, your 1 to 7 ratings, whether you opened the terms, and how long each part took. Your typed answers, IP address, location and device details are never stored.
